# Pokemon: Gym Leader — Granular Timeline

Protagonist: Dylan, Rota Gym. Starter: Sprigatito→Floragato→Meowscarada. Built from full chapter-1 text plus real excerpts from chapters 5, 14, 19, 25, 52, 65, 76, 90, and 280, read together with the complete 565-chapter title run.

**Ch. 1–4 — Inheriting a joke.** Dylan wakes mid-transmigration on a picnic blanket next to a dazed Slowpoke, works out he's been reborn eight years younger with the same name and face, and inherits the Rota Gym from a father who left with nothing but a note about a honeymoon. His actual roster is a punchline — Sprigatito, Oddish, Goldeen, Slowpoke, Spearow — and online reviews call the gym a "trash gym" on par with a Cerulean still run by its underqualified sisters. He meets Queen Ilene of Cameran Palace almost immediately (Ch. 2), establishing her as the story's one consistent emotional anchor, and starts sizing up "two Pokemon" he actually rates as usable.

**Ch. 5–13 — First challenger, first system reward.** Gary Oak shows up as Dylan's actual first challenger; Dylan introduces his gimmick on the spot — "I'm a multi-type trainer who selects the type of my team based on the challenger's strengths" — and wins with a Poison-type showing that leaves Gary confused rather than humiliated. A wave of players leaving good reviews follows, and "your system has arrived" formalizes the game-like mechanic underpinning his growth. He wants Darkrai specifically as an early stated ambition (Ch. 11), well before it's plausible, which the text treats as foreshadowing rather than a joke.

**Ch. 14–23 — Selene and the Inspection Bureau.** Dylan reads up on League lore (Rota's canon tie to the "Lucario and the Mystery of Mew" movie, and idle musing about when Ash might realistically pass through) while fending off a formal inspection bureau visit — the League is watching his reputation climb and wants to verify it's real. New challengers keep the pressure on; a "when I emerge, I'll punch Lance" boast (Ch. 22) sets up the payoff two chapters later. Selene is introduced by Ch. 24 as a local girl who'll become his apprentice.

**Ch. 24–39 — Building toward Lance.** Selene formally joins the gym; Vermilion City and a "date" subplot round out Dylan's social life while his team quietly grows (Darkrai gets closer, a Toxapex and other Poison-types round out a themed roster). "The Future Legend" and "You've Been Chosen" (Ch. 29–30) are Darkrai's actual recruitment chapters. A wealthy-privilege subplot (Ch. 39) needles at class dynamics among trainers before the story returns to gym business.

**Ch. 40–51 — The "Dodo" chapter and its comedy.** Agony (Ch. 40) sets up real stakes before Ch. 42's Pikachu-vs-Drapion showcase battle. By Ch. 51, "Alola has a Tiger God" rumors are circulating about Dylan's reputation reaching other regions — but the single most load-bearing joke of the entire series lands here in-context: Champion Lance challenges Rota expecting an easy win with three Dragonites and gets swept on the spot by four improvised Alolan Ninetales running a hail team. A reporter interview afterward has Lance refuse to answer three separate follow-up questions, and the internet renames him "Dodo" — a nickname every future Lance appearance in the series calls back to.

**Ch. 52–65 — Group chat, Reggie, and Incineroar.** Officer Jenny formally adds Dylan to a "Kanto Gym Leaders Group Chat," where Pewter's Flint and Cerulean's Daisy react to the newcomer with a mix of curiosity and shade — Jenny publicly holds Dylan up as an example the Cerulean sisters should learn from. Dylan trains on Victory Road specifically to prep for Reggie (a Frontier-adjacent battler), loses his composure once ("You've let me down"), then wins Reggie's respect properly by Ch. 62. Litten evolves into Incineroar mid-training-arc (Ch. 65) after five days of dedicated work, described in-text as violent-but-not-a-bully in temperament, "gets fired up when facing strong opponents."

**Ch. 66–90 — Lillie, Zinnia, Articuno, Team Rocket's first strike.** A worthless-gym insult (Ch. 66) sets up a Lillie cameo the very next chapter, quickly escalating into an Aster/Zinnia dragon-lore arc and Dylan's own Articuno acquisition. Chapters 70–85 are a rescue-and-jealousy comedy stretch ("Save Me!", "She Must Be Jealous," "I Won't Let You Go") layered directly on top of a genuine Team Rocket incident: four grunts get caught, roughed up (one bloodied), and interrogated on the spot by Dylan with Nidoking and Salazzle standing over them like bodyguards — he explicitly warns them off dying for Giovanni "it's not worth it" rather than just beating them. Ch. 90's "Story of Me and the Rota Gym" is a direct-address chapter where Dylan narrates the gym's history and his own arc so far, a structural breather before the pace picks back up.

**Ch. 91–113 — Villain Boss, Champion-Level claims, Groudon/Kyogre awakened.** A string of escalating challenges (Ch. 91–99, several framed as outright "fraud" or unfair by frustrated opponents) ends with someone declaring themselves "Champion-Level" directly to Dylan's face (Ch. 100). A childhood-friend reveal complicates a dragon-vs-dragon defeat two chapters later; a grandfather-summons-the-family beat brings extended cast in, and by Ch. 108 Dylan is being asked outright to "go awaken Groudon and Kyogre" — a new type gets added to his roster as a direct result, and a ghost-type "robbery" scare turns out to be exactly that, a robbery, not a haunting.

**Ch. 114–150 — N, Arceus's build-up, "just mere pebbles."** A long philosophical stretch runs through Unova's N ("They Don't Need to Evolve!", Ch. 117–118) alongside Dylan quietly assembling nearly every prior legendary he's fought into an actual team. A mother subplot (Ch. 140) and an unusually cryptic run of one-word/short titles (141–149: "WHO?!!!", "How strange," "Merely an empty shell") signal a genuinely tense mystery arc rather than a straightforward battle stretch, resolving in a "Save Me!!" cliffhanger.

**Ch. 151–186 — Unova's dragons, then Kitakami.** A "legal 2v1" battle format twist opens this block; a redacted-profanity chapter (163, "You ****") and a run of near-blank one-word titles (166–170: "Dragon," "Dragon 2," "Dragon 3," "Dragon 4," "(=_=)") mark an intense, almost wordless dragon-training montage. Leah is introduced by Ch. 175 (the same character the task material calls "Selene, later Leah" — likely a mid-story rename/relocalization). A Pidgey-and-pig comedy beat gives way abruptly to a full Kitakami arc (181–186): Bloodmoon lore, a lonely widow afraid of isolation, and a "We Can Be Partners" resolution that plays the Ogerpon/Loyal-Three material as emotional reconciliation, echoing the same beat used in the Ranger's sister story.

**Ch. 187–214 — Area Zero, Terapagos, and the aftermath of "closing the gym."** Someone challenges Dylan "as a Champion" outright; Area Zero and Terapagos both get folded into his collection across a tight run of chapters, alongside a genuinely dark stretch (206–208: "DYLAN!!!", "???", "I Never Want to Encounter Dylan Again") that reads as a real crisis rather than comedy, serious enough that "Rota Gym Is Closed Today" becomes an actual plot beat — the first time the gym itself stops operating mid-story.

**Ch. 215–232 — Ho-Oh, "I'm Your Dad!", and Dylan's first Mega Evolution.** A "Golden Legend" winner subplot, a family-reveal comedy chapter ("I'm Your Dad!"), and Ho-Oh's own dream/backstory all lead into a proper legendary showdown where Dylan Mega Evolves on-page for the first time (Ch. 229) in front of a crowd that immediately confirms what they'd suspected ("I KNEW IT!!!"). Selene sets off on her own independent journey right after, mirroring the "apprentice grows up and leaves" beat used elsewhere in the RockyRoad891 stories.

**Ch. 233–262 — Yveltal, Xerneas, and Giovanni's real threat begins.** A disguise plot and a fandom-crush subplot give way to a genuinely dark run (236–247) about being "archenemy of evil" and confronting someone who wants Dylan to teach them power for its own sake. Yveltal is awakened and confronted directly across several chapters; by Ch. 262, Giovanni is explicitly playing his "final trump card."

**Ch. 263–300 — Mewtwo, Rota's siege, Team Rainbow Rocket's debut.** Dylan insists on personally fighting Mewtwo rather than delegating it; the immediate aftermath is a full-on siege of Rota itself, with Blue, Selene ("Rota's New Member"), and the wider cast all pulled into direct defense of the town (268–276: "Invincible Ahead," "Help Me!", "Where is Dylan?!", "My Bro Dylan Is Unstoppable!"). A Caitlin arc (280, "Come Experience") has Dylan run an all-Water-type showcase specifically for her benefit, framed as generous hospitality rather than a real test. The block ends with Ultra Beasts appearing and "Team Rainbow Rocket" (an alternate-universe Giovanni faction) making its first appearance.

**Ch. 301–345 — Necrozma, Ultra Megalopolis, then Calyrex and the White Dragon.** Dylan is sent specifically to "go defeat that Necrozma," with Lance ("the Dragon Master") and Blue both drawn into Ultra-Space politics; Giovanni's defection subplot with Silver runs in parallel. By Ch. 327 Dylan duels Leon directly as "a battle between two champions," and Calyrex enters the story soon after — "I Am No Lord of Time!" is Calyrex correcting a mistaken assumption about its own domain, a small but deliberate lore-accuracy joke. The stretch closes on Eternatus being hunted down and successfully Eternamax'd and caught in a two-chapter climax.

**Ch. 346–376 — Kubfu, Iris's Kyurem, and the White Dragon's real test.** Kubfu is introduced as "The Furious Divine Avatar," followed by a run of chapters where Dylan's signature "White Dragon" (a Dragonite/dragon-type ace referenced by nickname, not species, throughout this stretch) is repeatedly asked to personally vouch for or fight alongside allies rather than just battle solo — Kyurem specifically needs its backup (Ch. 365), and Iris gets a rare non-humiliating rematch offer ("Let's Have a Friendly Match! Next Time, I Promise") rather than an outright loss.

**Ch. 377–420 — N's philosophy, Yveltal/Xerneas resolved, the World Cup begins.** N gets a genuine ideological confrontation ("Should N Just Flip the Table?") before Zekrom is talked into fighting on Dylan's behalf. Diantha's Kalos arc runs through Ch. 386–400, ending with Dylan's second Fairy-type Pokémon and Yveltal fully resolved as "the wing of death" is put down rather than merely delayed. Arceus, Dialga, and Palkia are all introduced back-to-back by Ch. 437–440, and a formal "World Cup Tournament" begins at Ch. 418 with Team Rainbow Rocket immediately striking again to disrupt it.

**Ch. 421–470 — Steven, Cynthia, and "is Champion Dylan even human?"** A dense run of champion-vs-champion exhibition matches (Steven vs. Cynthia at Ch. 444, framed explicitly as "because they're both champions," not because Dylan set it up) surrounds an escalating series of "how many of Dylan's aces can [X] defeat?" chapters that consistently answer "not enough." Rainbow Giovanni is revealed as Giovanni's own evil alternate-self around Ch. 454–457, complicating the "is Giovanni actually reformed" thread the story has been building since the Petalburg-adjacent chapters. The block closes with an "Eight Strongest Trainers in the World" framing becoming explicit series lore (Ch. 468).

**Ch. 471–524 — Treasures of Ruin, Arceus rematch, and the story mocking its own premise.** The four Treasures of Ruin converge on Dylan's roster in sequence; Arceus itself is fought and beaten a second time (Ch. 488, "Arceus, You're Defeated!"), prompting an in-world petition to literally ban Dylan from competing (Ch. 491) — the story naming its own escalation problem out loud as a joke rather than hiding it. Ash is written out of active competition around Ch. 500 via a direct author's note, clearing narrative space for Red, Steven, and Leon to anchor the remaining chapters without needing to route everything through Ash's canon timeline.

**Ch. 525–565 — The Eight Masters and the finale.** A dense tournament-bracket run (the top four decided by Ch. 538, Blue eliminated by Ch. 539) builds through a Hisui-flavored ghost-illusion gauntlet (540–545) into the finals proper (Ch. 554). The last twenty chapters trade off short, punchy battle-reaction titles ("This Damn Pokémon!", "You Did a Great Job," "Because It's Strong!") before Dylan wins outright in Ch. 565 ("I Am Dylan, the Master of the Rota Gym — Finale"), the author steps out of story-voice to thank readers directly, and the very last posted chapter is nothing but Dylan's complete final Pokémon roster, presented as a closing scoreboard rather than a scene.
