# Pokemon: A New Path (D) — Granular Timeline

**Calendar stamps:** use [`Stories/Story 1/Story 1 References/Dates.md`](../Stories/Story%201/Story%201%20References/Dates.md) (League years × chapter). Story 1 beat timeline: [`Timeline.md`](../Stories/Story%201/Story%201%20References/Timeline.md).

Built from actual chapter text (fanfiction.net ch.1, chereads "Past Events[2]") plus the [1]-[40] and [352]-[796] chapter-title runs, read as connected scene-beats. Bracket numbers [N] are the story's own true chronological numbering.

**[1]–[3] — Death, inheritance, ramen.** D (old life) dies of overwork and wakes as a same-named man who has just lost the only family he had. The original D's own history gets laid out immediately: born in Paldea, moved to Hoenn as a child, orphaned by his parents' illness, drifted for years never once catching a Pokémon, and finally came home at twenty to die alone on his grandfather's abandoned ranch. New-D's first real scene is 2 a.m., a bare kitchen, a pack of instant ramen oversalted into inedibility — and a faint glowing UI he recognizes from his old-world job: a cooking-simulator interface, except here the dishes actually grant stat boosts and abilities.

**[4]–[8] — Koraidon and the ranch's first real member.** A neighbor brings a Cyclizar to help around the property, framed as simple kindness before D grasps what he's actually been handed. Wandering the nearby forest, he finds Koraidon alone — not fought, not caught, just present — and it chooses to stay, drawn in the same way every later Pokémon in the story will be: by the cooking, and by D simply treating it like a person rather than a tool. By [8] it's explicitly framed as "God of the Past" living quietly as a farmhand.

**[9]–[19] — Small domestic beats.** A day by the river, gifts and discoveries, a Caterpie whose whole subplot is a small child-Pokémon's determination to prove itself useful despite being weak, and the ranch finally getting a name. A chicken coop goes up over several chapters (Building a Chicken Coop, then "The Coop" as its own follow-up), and the first real culinary experiment lands — Caterpie gets pitted directly against a wild Delibird in a comedy-scaled skirmish that's really about Caterpie's confidence, not a real threat.

**[20]–[30] — Alcremie, Smoliv, and the ranch's first public reputation.** Alcremie and Smoliv join and immediately become the ranch's signature dessert-and-oil producers; a caramel cream berry shortcake becomes a minor local sensation. Unexpected visitors start showing up specifically because word is spreading, and by [29]-[30] D is deliberately choosing to keep growing rather than staying small — "The Book of Scarlet," a recurring in-universe reference text, gets introduced here as D's own research tool.

**[31]–[40] — Nacli, Dragonite, and the first real family unit.** A videophone call and a "familiarity" chapter deepen D's bond with the household before the Pokémon of Casseroya Lake introduce a mysterious new arrival: Nacli, who joins at [35] and becomes a running long-term evolution project. New ranch ventures follow immediately, and — the single biggest addition of this whole opening stretch — a Dragonite egg is acquired and "Dragonite Island" is established as its own dedicated home base by [40], set up by an S-tier milk porridge recipe that (per the chapter title) is specifically what makes the island viable.

**[41]–[54] — Ranch routines solidify.** Skiddo joins; milking, deliveries, and dragon-tail training become recurring chores rather than one-off events — D starts a genuine delivery business using Dragonite as courier ([45]-[49]), which the story treats as a real working relationship, not a gimmick. A letter and a friend's visit round out the emotional beats before payday closes the block on a note of the ranch actually becoming financially real.

**[55]–[70] — Butterfly, blossoms, and a mysterious intruder.** Butterfree joins during a cherry-blossom reception that draws outside guests; a spicy boiled-pork-slices recipe becomes its own small subplot. Combee and Pawmi join in quick succession; a mysterious intruder plotline gets set up and then defused rather than escalated into a real threat — consistent with the story's pattern of de-fanging anything that looks like conflict.

**[71]–[80] — Palafin, Teddiursa, and Miltank's growing chaos.** Palafin and Teddiursa both join here, with Teddiursa specifically framed as "the little honey thief" — a running gag about it stealing sweets that eventually becomes an actual character trait rather than a joke that fades. Miltank's introduction ("Obesity," a self-deprecating title about its own body type) leads into a Frisbee-game bonding chapter and, by [80], a literal typhoon threatening the property.

**[81]–[100] — Storm recovery into the Spring Competition.** The typhoon's eye passes and repair work follows immediately — Dragonite's own aspiration to help rebuild gets a dedicated chapter. A Spring Cattle-and-Sheep Competition becomes the first real public contest the ranch enters, with Baby Bear (Teddiursa) getting cast as an unlikely "Hero" mascot mid-competition. By [100], D is explicitly "preparing to leave" — the ranch's first trip away from home.

**[101]–[115] — Dragonite Island revisited, Glaseado Mountain.** Calyrex is introduced here for the first time (a "home of strength and bonds" framing rather than a battle), and the group returns to Dragonite Island for a bonding-focused visit before heading to Glaseado Mountain, where a strange trainer, a secret ice cave, and Glaceon's own evolution (via Ice Stone) round out the trip. The visible record breaks off here — [115] "Good Friends" is the last chapter recoverable before a roughly 236-chapter gap in what's hosted online.

**[352]–[363] — Picking back up: Flutter Mane, Giratina's first spark.** The household already includes Flutter Mane and Misdreavus by the time the record resumes. A Dragonite egg and a contest chapter lead into Koraidon voicing genuine worries about its own place in the family, resolved via an amusement-park trip to Alto Mare where Latias and Latios make a guest appearance. "Joining forces with Giratina?" (Ch. [362]) is the exact moment Giratina's attachment to D starts being played as more than casual interest — the very next chapter has "The King of Hammers" (Garganacl) born from Nacli's years-long evolution project finally paying off.

**[364]–[380] — Eevee family begins, Rayquaza.** Eevee joins, and a dedicated "Eeveelutions" chapter starts the household's long project of raising every evolution line. Rayquaza gets a full heroic introduction ("The Heavenly Hero," then "A Call to the Sky") tied directly to Dragonite's own training — the dragon lines and the sky-legendary arcs are deliberately interwoven rather than separate threads. Grusha (an Ice-type gym figure) visits mid-stretch, giving "a warm hearth for an icy heart" — the story's habit of solving a character's coldness with hospitality rather than a battle.

**[381]–[400] — Desert fossils, Gholdengo's birth.** Geeta's talent-recruitment plan brings outside scouting attention to the ranch for the first time; Scyther joins during a desert chase sequence that leads into unearthing an actual buried ancient city and fossils beneath the sand. Gholdengo is born from a Gimmighoul the ranch had been quietly feeding — treated as a genuine surprise birth rather than a planned capture — and an "Eevee Nursery" formalizes the household's growing Eeveelution project with three specific Eevee siblings.

**[401]–[430] — Dragon Brothers, Giratina's online fame.** A "Dragon Brothers" subplot deepens Dragonite's own family life; a Time Paradox chapter and a Tropius arrival round out the middle stretch before Giratina's courtship of D goes fully public — "A God's Online Fury" and "Giratina's Internet Debut" are the exact chapters where footage of Giratina at the ranch leaks and it becomes a genuine internet celebrity, treated by the story as an escalating, slightly absurd romantic-comedy beat rather than a crisis. Professor Oak visits in person and is repeatedly shocked by what he finds.

**[431]–[461] — Dragonite Express, Mewtwo joins the chat, Treasures foreshadowed.** A formal delivery service ("Dragonite Express") launches alongside a viral "Life Goat Milk" product; Mewtwo joins the recurring Legendary Chat Group here, and Steven Stone personally requests a meeting. The chapter titled "A Rancher with Too Many Legends" ([451]) is the story explicitly naming its own premise as a joke, right before Jirachi's search-and-recruit arc begins.

**[462]–[483] — Jirachi's choice, the Hoenn incident, Rayquaza the Hero (again).** Jirachi chooses to stay after a genuine search-and-bonding arc; almost immediately a real crisis breaks out in Hoenn, escalating into a "Battle of Gods" that Rayquaza personally intervenes in as "The Great Hero." A masked-savior subplot and a Mega Stone discovery round out the block, which closes on fireworks marking the end of a story "volume" — a formal structural break the author uses to mark act transitions.

**[484]–[520] — Calyrex vs. Calyrex, the Treasures of Ruin arrive one by one.** A haunted-forest chapter leads into an actual duel between two different Calyrex-adjacent forces before the real Treasures-of-Ruin arc begins in earnest: Ting-Lu, Chi-Yu, Wo-Chien, and Chien-Pao each get their own dedicated introduction chapter, each drawn to the ranch by cooking or land rather than combat. Kubfu arrives mid-stretch and starts training under the ranch's supervision (sparring directly with Cyclizar for practice), and a Naranja-style "trial" briefly tests the ranch's residents formally.

**[521]–[560] — Alola again, birds, Naranja's Grass Trial returns.** Articuno negotiates an explicit price for its cooperation (a rare beat of a legendary being transactional rather than won over emotionally); a second Alola trip surfaces Moltres and reopens "the Green Grass Trial" the ranch had encountered earlier. Clair (Blackthorn's Dragon-type gym leader) visits for a proper showdown, and Tapu Koko fights Koraidon across two dedicated chapters before a Z-Crystal changes the terms of their rivalry.

**[561]–[590] — Arceus descends, the Rise of Darkrai / Giratina and the Sky Warrior.** "A World Without You" (three parts) is the story's most dramatic false-loss beat before Arceus itself descends directly. Immediately after, the story adapts two Pokémon films back to back: Dialga and Palkia's collision (from "The Rise of Darkrai") gets defused with Kyogre's help at D's request, and Giratina genuinely loses control in the "Sky Warrior" adaptation that follows — the household has to talk it back down rather than fight it, with Rayquaza and Mewtwo drawn into the aftermath.

**[591]–[625] — Marriage, wedding, home.** A dedicated "Wedding Day" chapter marks D's marriage; "Pokemon: Home" (two parts) closes out a full story volume around the new household. A Mysterious Labyrinth subplot and a reunion with the Legendary Beast trio run in parallel with a lighter Giovanni subplot — he wants to make friends, not fight, which the story plays completely straight. By [612]-[613] a "spark of new life" leads directly into D himself being appointed a regional Gym Leader, treated as incidental recognition rather than a new personal goal.

**[626]–[660] — Live-streaming, Hoopa, the Ice Dragon.** The ranch starts live-streaming its goods for sale; a returning Green Grass Trial and a Gogoat-vs-Charizard rematch round out the competitive material. Raikou and Victini both join around here, alongside a "sad story" chapter that gives one of them real emotional backstory before a Wish-granting subplot (two parts) and a Meltan side-arc about a tiny thief stealing metal at night close out the block.

**[661]–[691] — Blueberry Academy, Kyurem, Elara's conception.** D formally enrolls in or teaches at Blueberry Academy for a multi-chapter stretch (his own "class," two parts); Sylveon fights Kyurem across three dedicated chapters, and Koraidon gets its own solo bout with Kyurem immediately after. The block ends on "Nine Months" (two parts) — the pregnancy leading to the birth of D's daughter, Elara.

**[692]–[720] — Elara's infancy, Hoopa's Prison Bottle.** "Papa" is D's first chapter as a father; Elara's earliest adventures get their own dedicated two-part chapter. Hoopa is freed from and then returns to a literal Prison Bottle across a three-part arc that reads as morally complicated rather than a simple rescue, followed by a "the bad guys are gone" resolution and a formal expansion of the ranch's "Hero Expedition Team" by one new permanent member. A Lumiose Conference and Aether Foundation subplot (Lusamine included) close the block.

**[721]–[760] — The World Championship.** D competes directly: a Tobias rematch (two parts), a Latias-vs-Koraidon and Kyurem-vs-Cresselia pair of legendary showdowns, and a Calyrex-vs-Deoxys spectacle lead into a Flint rematch (two parts) and a proper quarterfinal bracket. Red is introduced as a genuine rival here — his own discovery subplot leads into him meeting, then battling, Ash directly (two dedicated chapters neither about D at all, a rare authorial choice to let two other characters carry a beat). D then beats Steven and Leon in sequence before winning the whole tournament outright at [760], "Champion!!!"

**[761]–[796] — Darkest Day, Elara grows up, series finale.** A Darkest Day crisis and a "Galar, your king is back" cameo round out the post-championship comedown before the story shifts almost entirely onto Elara: her first Pokémon, her exclusive teacher, a trip to Kitakami framed as "the Legendary Girl" conquering it in miniature, and a running thread about how much D dotes on her ("He is my dad!", told from her perspective). Terapagos and Volcarona both get folded in as Elara's own companions rather than D's. The story closes on a long, quiet emotional run — "Time is like water," "A Spring Without You," a two-part "I Will Always Love You" — before ending flatly and deliberately on [796], "Every End Is a New Beginning."
