# Pokemon: Spiritual Master (Johan) — Granular Timeline

Built from full chapter-1 text (fanfiction.net) and freewebnovel's complete 1–40 title run, plus real excerpts read directly from chapters 97, 109, 221, 306, 321, 364, 407, 426, 553, 609, 610, 612, 617, 619–621, 654, and 808–818. This story's middle third (roughly 41–800) is only sparsely verified — flagged honestly where the record thins out.

**Ch. 1–5 — A very ordinary afternoon.** Johan transmigrates already employed: an instructor running a Pokémon-psychology consultation room at Kanaz Academy in Hoenn. The opening scene is domestic, not dramatic — he's grinding coffee with an Onix grinder, tamping it with a Graveler, brewing it in a Blastoise machine, reading his own paper on Pokémon personality and battle performance while his Gastly hovers nearby. "Professor Cozmo" and a "remote login" gag (Ch. 2–3) establish the system/interface conceit early, and by Ch. 5 the tone is fully set: low-stakes, dryly funny, therapist-not-fighter.

**Ch. 6–17 — Steven Stone, consultation, danger in the alley.** Steven Stone is Johan's first real "client" — a consultation chapter treats him like an actual patient, not a cameo. Watch-your-back and sense-of-danger chapters (9–10) escalate into a genuine alley confrontation: Johan gets poisoned (Ch. 13) and has Gastly use Hypnosis on an attacker (Ch. 14) in a fight that's played seriously rather than as slapstick, resolving with "the greedy Gastly" getting fed as a reward and an "∞ Energy" chapter hinting at a deeper system mechanic. Ch. 17's "completely ordinary" class is ironic — it's anything but.

**Ch. 18–24 — Mewtwo's first contact.** "They Call Me Mewtwo" is the exact chapter where Johan starts remotely counseling Mewtwo through a chat-style interface — the seed the entire 818-chapter story eventually pays off in its final chapter. Gastly gets two dedicated character chapters right after ("Gastly, Gastly!", "I, Gastly, Am the God of Alley Fights!") establishing it as an actual street-fighting force, not just comic relief, before a robbery/street-battle stretch and a "Money Tree" chapter round out Johan's growing reputation as someone dangerous to underestimate.

**Ch. 25–29 — Cynthia arrives.** "Sinnoh, Cynthia" is a dedicated introduction chapter — she isn't folded into an ensemble scene, she gets her own full entrance. A four-part "Autumn Outing" immediately follows, establishing the pattern the whole series will repeat for its central relationship: long, slow, chapter-spanning hangouts rather than plot-driven encounters.

**Ch. 30–40 — Heatran and Mt. Chimney.** A genuine environmental-crisis stretch: "The Change at Mt. Chimney" leads into two warning chapters and Heatran's own reveal (Ch. 32), with a former Elite Four member and N both surfacing as complicating factors. Gastly's training method gets explicitly interrogated as unfair ("Dirty Tactics? Not Really") before it visibly matures ("My Gastly is Growing Up"), and a run of chapters about "Toxic" as both a move and a training philosophy (37–40) closes out the block with Johan defending his own methods on the record.

**Scattered confirmed beats, ch. 44–115 (record thins here):** Ch. 44, "Mewtwo's Tears," is where Mewtwo's actual tragic creation-story gets told properly, deepening the Ch. 18 setup. Ch. 54 brings in an Alolan Vulpix as Johan's second long-term partner. Ch. 65 is Gastly's formal evolution into Haunter. Ch. 79 touches Regigigas/temple lore. Ch. 97 ("Being Johan's Pokémon Is Pure Happiness," a BONUS chapter) has Cynthia visiting Johan's home for the first time, genuinely curious rather than performing — she notices an egg he's carrying everywhere (a gift from Joseph Stone, contents unknown even to the giver) that later hatches into Kubfu.

**Ch. 106, 109 — Giovanni's first real consultation, Wallace's exhibition.** Ch. 106 is Giovanni's own introduction as a client rather than an enemy — the "I am Giovanni" framing plays him as guarded but genuinely seeking help. Ch. 109, "Wallace and Sharpedo," is an exhibition match where the point is explicitly to perform rather than win — Wallace's Contest-trained flair gets contrasted directly against a more results-driven fighting style, with the narration noting Wallace is more than just "a trainer of beauty" underneath the showmanship.

**Ch. 221 — Viridian Forest strategy chapter.** "Secrets of the Earth" is a mechanically dense chapter: a Rock Slide/Assist-move combo pushes a Chansey's evasion and stat boosts to extreme levels, and the fallout is comedic rather than triumphant — every Nurse Joy and every wild Pokémon in Viridian Forest is described as losing sleep over what Johan pulled off. A meta-joke about an in-universe anime production team sending Johan a rough-cut episode for feedback (with a specific compliment about Jessie's acting) shows the story periodically breaking the fourth wall about its own source material.

**Ch. 306 — Battle Frontier, Kubfu's public debut.** Framed around the Battle Frontier's "Guts Symbol," this is Kubfu's first big showcase battle since hatching from the Ch. 97 egg — the story treats it as a coming-of-age chapter for the Pokémon specifically, not for Johan.

**Ch. 321 — Ecruteak Gym, a friendly rivalry.** Morty invites Johan to Ecruteak explicitly as a low-stakes sparring session ("it doesn't have to be a Gengar battle"), but Charcadet volunteers itself against Morty's Banette anyway, wanting to prove itself on its own terms rather than let Johan pick a safer matchup. The two Gengars — Morty's and Johan's — trash-talk each other's trainers mid-fight in a bit of comic business that doubles as genuine mutual respect between the two men.

**Ch. 364 — Volkner's bet.** Johan and Flint deliberately cut a gym's power as a prank before Volkner even knows he's being challenged; Tinkaton smashing a door becomes a running callback (with an aside noting it had recently "smashed a Regigigas into the ground," implying an off-page legendary fight the reader never sees directly). The chapter plays the whole thing as an elaborate practical joke that happens to also be a real gym challenge.

**Ch. 407, 426 — The Lily of the Valley Conference.** Ch. 407 opens Johan's entry into Sinnoh's actual regional league — the story's most conventional tournament arc. Ch. 426 is the finals, against Tobias, the canon anime trainer famous for owning both a Darkrai and a Latios — treated as a genuine test of Johan's strategic ability rather than a foregone conclusion.

**Ch. 553 — Alola exhibition, Ceruledge's debut.** "Ceruledge: Henshin!" has Johan face an Alolan gym figure named Molayne (Alolan Dugtrio and Skarmory on his side); nearly the entire mainline Alola cast — Kiawe, Sophocles, Mallow, Lana — watches from the stands and openly wants Johan to guest-lecture at their school rather than just admiring the battle. Cynthia, Flint, Lance, and Steven are all in the crowd too, each privately wanting their own shot at him. Johan advances to a "Top 16" bracket, confirming a background tournament structure the story periodically dips into.

**Ch. 609–621 — Reconciling with Giovanni, confronting Arceus.** A dense, mostly comic run: "Through Fire and Water, Boss Giovanni!" reframes Giovanni fully as an ally by this point in the story; "Who Still Isn't a Top-Level Trainer?" and "It Moved! It Moved!" (610, 612) escalate a training or discovery beat that isn't fully clear from the excerpt alone; "I'm Shudao Mountain!" (617) suggests a location-based challenge; and the block climaxes with Arceus itself being challenged directly ("Arceus, Let's Battle!"), a frantic call for backup ("Calling in Help Like Crazy!"), and Johan admitting he nearly threw an actual Poké Ball at the creator deity out of habit (621) — the joke being that after 600 chapters of collecting legendaries as friends, his first instinct around an even bigger one is still "catch it."

**Ch. 654 — Kyogre enters the plot.** "Kyogre: Who's Calling Me?" (a two-in-one chapter) folds Kyogre into the story's ongoing web of legendary relationships, consistent with the sea-temple/Manaphy material referenced elsewhere in the series' broader synopsis.

**GAP, ch. 655–807:** not individually verified in this pass. This stretch covers, per the series' own late-chapter framing, the build-up to the "Eight Masters" world tournament — expect further gym-tour and Team Plasma/Team Flare material here based on the story's established rhythm, but nothing below this line should be treated as confirmed without reading the actual chapters.

**Ch. 808–818 — The Eight Masters finale, in full.** Beedrill Mega Evolves in Johan's hands for the first time (808) heading into the tournament's late rounds. Johan defeats Giovanni in the finals at Ch. 810, "Champion, Johan!" — the payoff of a relationship that started as a wary first consultation back at Ch. 106. A legendary exhibition against Red follows (813–814, "VS Red! Legendary Battle!", "Red: Even More Unreasonable Than Me!"), and the story's real emotional climax isn't Johan's own title — it's Gengar getting a dedicated two-chapter spotlight (815–816, "My Shining Debut!" and an exclusive Z-Move), the partner introduced in chapter one finally getting the biggest moment in the book. Ch. 817 is a quiet coda (Johan formally recognized as Hoenn's League President), and the series closes on Ch. 818, "Mewtwo, Captured! (The End)" — Johan finally catching the Pokémon whose tragic backstory he first heard, secondhand, in Ch. 18 and Ch. 44, closing the loop the very first arc opened.
