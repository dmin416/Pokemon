# Pokemon: The Chosen Ranger — Granular Timeline

Protagonist: Summer Leaf / Xia Ye. Starter: Grookey→Thwackey→Rillaboom. Built from actual chapter text (fanmtl.com chapters 1, 6, 62, 93, 209; chereads.com/webnovel.com excerpts for 40, 75, 102–114, 145, 244–253) plus the full 1–349 chapter-title run, read as connected scene-beats rather than compressed into arcs.

**Ch. 1–5 — Landing.** Summer wakes mid-transmigration in a flying taxi over Galar, groggy enough that the driver has to shake him awake. Stepping out, the first thing he sees is a black Corviknight — his brain immediately clocks "flying taxi, steel-armored crow, I've traveled to Pokémon world, Galar region." He meets a starving Grookey that's lost its drumstick, shares half his bread with it, and a system voice starts narrating conditional traits in real time (`Grassmaker`, `Leadership — become the leader of more than ten Pokémon`) — the mechanic is explicitly framed as *earn the trait by actually doing the thing*, not spend points. He sets up a cabin, meets trainers in Spike Town, and gets his first real system reward tied to "Variety Optimization" — a Rotom negotiation quirk that lets him talk down deals.

**Ch. 6–11 — Piers, and the first recruits.** Piers holds a concert in town; Summer attends as an outsider still learning the social map (this is where Marnie is properly introduced, not just named). He starts special-training the Grookey on wood-branch-thorn drills, and a second Corvisquire ("Blue Jay") joins the training group specifically because it wants to keep up.

**Ch. 12–19 — The poacher and the gym-challenge frame.** A Pokémon Hunter is caught trying to work the forest's edges; Summer runs him off, which is the first time the story frames him as protector rather than just caretaker. He expands the forest's planted area with Gracidea grass (Shaymin-adjacent flora), picks up new residents, and beats Marnie in a proper battle for the first time — not a training spar, an actual contested fight, which the text treats as a status marker. Chapter 19 stages a mock "Gym Challenge Opening Ceremony" for the forest itself, half PR stunt, half real test.

**Ch. 20–29 — Misty Grassland distortion, first evolution.** A time-space distortion drops the forest into a misty grassland; Grookey wins a decisive fight here and evolves into Thwackey mid-chapter (Ch. 25, "Victory and Evolution") — the evolution lands specifically because of a battle it needed to win, not a level threshold. The following chapters ("King of Lakes," "Bet and Transformation") escalate into a Feebas/Milotic-adjacent subplot at a lake, with a literal wager riding on the outcome, and a two-sword Sirfetch'd-style duel ("the sudden stab of a wooden branch, two-sword style") shows Thwackey experimenting with dual-stick combat.

**Ch. 30–39 — Misty Lake politics.** "Queen of the Lake" and "Lord of Mist Lake" are two separate NPC Pokémon with rival claims over the same body of water; Summer gets pulled into brokering it ("Mist Lake Renovation Plan") rather than picking a side by force. A "menacing guest" tests him, Thwackey spars an actual wild Pangoro, and a slice-of-life stretch ("A Day with Marnie") cements her as a recurring presence rather than a one-off rival. Team Dawn — a recurring trio of side-characters — gets formally introduced around Ch. 38.

**Ch. 40–51 — First Hisui distortion.** The forest is pulled into the Crimson Mirelands (ancient Sinnoh). Summer recognizes the terrain immediately and starts asking after "the Diamond Clan." A girl worshipping "the Queen of Ancient Pass" and a pair of strong locals test him in "Shazhou" (a walled town). A lonely Dragonair-line Pokémon ("the Lonely Sticky Dragon") gets a redemption-style arc resolved by Ch. 44's "truth of the test," and the visit closes with gifts from the local Diamond Clan and a farewell flower celebration before the distortion reverses.

**Ch. 52–66 — Ranger politics, then the Spirit World.** Summer attends a Ranger Exchange Meeting and meets fellow rangers Annie, Viola, and Mido — the first time "forest ranger" is shown as a real profession with peers, not just Summer's personal job. A Nuzleaf-led "Long Nose Leaf Legion" and a solitary Sirfetch'd swordsman round out the local cast. Then a second distortion opens a door specifically into the spirit world through Lost Light Forest: Ch. 62's actual scene is a girl named Popura, daughter of a Dance-Gym-owning mother (Eleph Cappe, standing in for Opal), whose Fairy-type Pokémon evolves out of gratitude after Popura risks her life to save it — a real emotional beat, not a fight — right as the system flags that "the door to the spiritual world is about to open." Chapters 63–66 have Summer and companions physically travel into that spirit world and meet its Gatekeeper, coming back with gifts and a rescued lost soul.

**Ch. 67–76 — Corviknight's arc.** The "Blue Jay" Corvisquire explicitly tells Summer "I want to become stronger," and the chapters that follow are a dedicated strength-arc for it — a "victory equation" battle, then a fight to actually conquer/tame a wild Steel-Armored Crow rival, unlocking its potential. In parallel, Hatterene (the "Witch of the Dream Forest") starts actively trying to befriend Summer's group rather than being fought, and an Applin evolves into the rarer golden Dipplin mid-arc.

**Ch. 77–90 — First Alola distortion.** Akala Island. A post-disaster town is short on hope; Summer's arrival and Rillaboom's climactic evolution (during an actual fight against a rampaging "King Kong Ape" threat, not a quiet moment) turns the tide. An Island Guardian hands down a commission, a rainforest "Overlord of the Lake" gets subdued, and the arc closes with a dedicated flower-field farewell to Tapu Bulu ("Kapu Diedie") before the trip ends.

**Ch. 91–100 — Kitakami prelude.** Two "Research Report on Plants from Another World" chapters function as a breather/lore-dump before the plot restarts. Ch. 93's actual scene: two visiting women, Fusha and Xiaolu, arrive at Sunset Forest specifically because Fusha's overpowered "King Kong" Pokémon once humiliated Xiaolu in a competition, and Xiaolu is nervous about Ghost-types after repeated losses — they're holding a small Darkrai wood-carving when the ranger assistant senses something is coming. A tea-Pokémon dignity battle and traveling photographers round out the chapter before a distortion opens toward Beishang Township (Kitakami).

**Ch. 101–114 — Kitakami / Ogerpon, in full.** Investigation into "the Story of Sanbaoban" (the Loyal Three) opens the arc; Summer meets an introverted boy who worships ghosts (Kieran) and fights his sister figure Dan Yu (Carmine) directly. The mask lore unfolds over four dedicated chapters ("Ghosts and Masks," "Mask to Ghost," "Taking Back the Mask" I & II) — retrieving the mask costs real prices (wisdom, then power and beauty, per the chapter titles), and a "Mysterious Master" revises the treasure companions' allegiance mid-arc. Kieran's own challenge to Summer is the emotional climax before "no more legends of evil spirits" resolves the historical wrong and Kitakami sends the group home with a parting gift.

**Ch. 115–125 — Aftermath and Galar's undercurrent.** Direct fallout from Kitakami — facing residual "evil thoughts," a gift chapter, then the Blood Moon's past (Ursaluna lore) and a "Moon Bear" finding a new home. A Pawniard-type "Ju Dao Soldier" spars Marnie specifically. The stretch closes ominously on "the undercurrent of Galar" — a deliberate tension-build before Eternatus enters the picture.

**Ch. 126–141 — Eternatus interrogated, then sealed threats.** A mutation experiment and an "unlucky uninvited guest" (Eternatus itself) get investigated directly; visiting karate prodigy Bea shows up mid-arc almost as a palate cleanser. An "elderly gathering" turns out to include serious hidden power. A second Hisui-adjacent blizzard distortion opens with real stakes — "disasters and man-made disasters," a Pokémon messenger tasked with suppressing them, a Wyrdeer-type "Leader in Blizzard," and a hunt for the "Sword of Disaster" (Chien-Pao) that ends in a divine sealing technique successfully containing it.

**Ch. 142–168 — Snowpoint & the Ranger Exam.** An inheritance-themed master/disciple journey, then a real slice-of-life run: a rock-type gym visitor (Roxie), a King-Kong-vs-Centipede sparring match, a rescued evil-ghost/wood-spirit choice, and a masked boy hiding his identity (Allister). Ch. 152 is the Giratina chapter proper — a full spirit-world team battle inside which Giratina itself arrives, prompting the group to fashion "a new gatekeeper" and a fresh mask. Pryce visits with his Articuno the night before a planned concert; a two-in-one "Feast of Viciousness" chapter turns violent before the bear tribe subplot resolves peacefully around a honey tree. Celebi finally speaks directly to Summer across two consecutive chapters — "Your name is Xia Ye" and "Sunset Forest is my home" — establishing Celebi as a resident, not a visitor. The exam itself (Ch. 164–168) is a real certification test under Kabu, ending with an egg as the reward.

**Ch. 169–186 — Second Hisui trip, Snowpoint Temple.** A distortion to "Pure White Frozen Earth" opens with a girl hunting medicine ingredients while a shared nightmare plagues everyone nearby. A leader of "Pearl Team" and a lost man tied to "New Moon Feathers" complicate the nightmare-mystery before a resurrected mythical giant (Regigigas) has to be fought back in a two-in-one chapter, followed immediately by a "Regichicas" (Regieleki) subplot and a Lake-trio negotiation ("I am good at making chains" — one of the lake spirits speaking in character). The trip winds down with village farewells and a quiet visitor scene involving a photo souvenir and — notably — someone traveling with an actual Darkrai as their trainer partner.

**Ch. 187–199 — The Pokémon Gang.** A villain trio (Oranguru/Honchkrow/Perrserker, framed here as "the Pokémon Gang") attacks outright, gets defeated over two chapters, and is formally disbanded in a third — the story's first fully self-contained good-guys-win-clean arc. Milcery evolves into Alcremie ("spin it, little fairy milk") right after, and a Fantasy Magician visitor gets genuine advice from Summer rather than a battle.

**Ch. 200–211 — Team Rocket's invasion, blow by blow.** Opens with a Wooloo-trainer challenge as a red herring before the real threat lands: a Celebi-capture plot triggers all-out "Resistance in the Sunset Forest," and three Team Rocket cadres fight the group in direct sequence across three consecutive chapters (Ch. 206–208, each literally titled "Three cadres fighting each other" parts 1–3). "Fanatic Followers" mop up, and the arc's actual military resolution — "The Battle Belongs to the Rangers, the End of the Remaining Team Rocket" — is a two-in-one chapter, followed by formal post-event tripartite talks between the rangers, Spikemuth, and the League.

**Ch. 212–235 — Reconstruction, Marnie's arc, Pecharunt.** Reward logistics (a literal "blind box" opening chapter), fulfilling promises to allied Pokémon groups, then Marnie's own dedicated return and training-plan arc (two-in-one chapters each) that culminates in an actual confession scene between her and Summer. In parallel, "the three treasure companions" (the Loyal Three, revisited) break fully free of old chains, and the mysterious "Ergui" (Pecharunt) gets investigated across a run of chapters that end with its tragic backstory ("the truth revealed about Momo's past") reframing it as a victim, not a threat, before it's welcomed in with two brand-new masks made specifically for it.

**Ch. 236–253 — Champion visit, then the Fields of Honor buildup.** Galar's actual League Champion (Leon) visits personally. A dragon-emperor "Ancient Family" gets investigated, Corviknight becomes an adoptive father to a rescued chick, and a video call brings in the thunder-and-lightning Witch subplot. Galar's ice specialists (Gordie and Melony) arrive one after another, culminating in an actual mother-son battle refereed by Summer. The chapters then pivot to Kai/Fields-of-Honor prep, a desert detour where a Galarian Moltres-type "Evil Bird" attacks and gets fought off in a two-in-one battle chapter.

**Ch. 254–271 — Mustard's trial, Ancient Galar knights begin.** Mustard ("Ma Shide") tests Summer directly across two consecutive two-in-one battle chapters; a new disciple (Xiong/Clara) arrives at Sunset Forest the same stretch and gets her own bonding scene with Marnie before formally starting training under Mustard's "so-called martial arts." A huge team battle at Weimei Forest closes the stretch, followed immediately by first contact with "the so-called knights of ancient Galar" and Summer's own guilt-tinged reflection ("I'm sorry that Pokémon is so powerful") about the imbalance his growing team represents.

**Ch. 272–300 — Sword & Shield heroes, then an exchange conference.** A full sword-battle between rival blade-Pokémon (Sirfetch'd-line) resolves with a literal transformation for the winner; the "Warrior of a Hundred Battles" title gets earned properly across a two-in-one chapter, followed by "reforging the sword and shield" itself as a legendary gift, and a farewell to the Sword/Shield hero Pokémon before an Energy-Factory infiltration subplot (mutation, dark-night resistance, a legend's reappearance) resolves in a two-in-one crisis-ending chapter starring a gambler and an actor as unlikely allies. The block ends on a full "Legend Hero Lecture Class" and an invitation from Galar's actual royal descendants.

**Ch. 301–328 — Crown Snowfield, the Regi-titans.** The Crown Snowfield arc is the story's densest "collect the ancient giants" stretch: a missing King of Plenty, a Crown Temple miracle, and a Ruins-of-Decision expedition surface Regieleki ("Thunder Giant") first, followed immediately by a father-daughter reunion subplot (a man who lost his memory, reunited with his Pokémon daughter) woven directly into the legendary-hunting rather than set apart from it. A Grass-Element Exchange Conference closes the block with two full "Grass Pokémon Experts" theory-and-practice chapters and a King-Kong-vs-Lizard-King sparring exhibition.

**Ch. 329–349 — Exchange Meeting aftermath, second Alola trip (ongoing).** Fifty-years-later reminiscence chapters give the mentors room to be curious about Summer rather than just impressed; a holy-swordsman battle turns a literal thorny vine stick into a "Thorny Vine Holy Sword." A second Alola invitation kicks off a beach-set arc: a Skeleton Team attacks, a "Shout Team" assembles in response, and a swimsuit-and-shadow subplot around a character named Mary runs alongside genuine beach-defense combat. The arc's last stretch (visitors "different from the past," an evil beast that devours everything, a protective phantom butterfly) is still unresolved as of Ch. 349, the latest chapter online — it ends mid-investigation, with "the Ultimate Hole That Tears the Sky Apart" as the most recent cliffhanger.

*(Chapters flagged in the earlier index as author's-note "leave notice" entries — 212, 244, 255, 269, 276, 298–299, 329, 343 — are skipped above since they carry no story content.)*
