# 1: Chapter 1: D

D woke up and opened his eyes.

Everything around him was quiet.

He watched as the pendulum of a grandfather clock swung from side to side with a gentle click.

He closed his eyes before taking a big breath.

This wasn't anything like his normal room.

Grinning, he realized this would be a new adventure.

Sitting up from his bed, he looked around.

He noticed this was an old-fashioned log cabin, though a bit on the smaller side.

The bed was against a corner with a small desk across the room.

The desk had a bulging backpack on top of it.

A yellowed calendar hung on the wall beside him.

Across the room stood a wooden cabinet.

Next to it sat a stove piled with black rocks and kitchenware like pots and pans.

D raised an eyebrow at the sight.

It had been a while since he had been in a coal-era world.

Given the materials of the backpack it was more likely he was in a cabin rather than the past.

D narrowed his eyes as his head began to throb.

Memories of this cabin overlapped with his own.

Images and memories of another life pressed in with them.

He saw images of parents he had never known.

He saw creatures that filled this world and lived with the people in it.

The first owner of this body was Naoki.

He came from Paldea before moving to Hoenn.

His parents died of illness there so he sold everything and wandered for years.

He never caught a Pokémon.

He did not want attachments.

A Taiwanese game developer named Chu Feng later entered that empty life and lived in this body as his own.

Chu Feng carried a deep depression with him.

He had yearned for another world and escaped crunch, shipping pressure, and loans by coming here.

He tried to live like a trainer for a while.

At twenty he finally came back to Paldea, to the ranch his late grandfather had left the first Naoki.

He was caught between the work that needed to be done on the farm and the joy and relaxation he felt from escaping responsibilities he had fled.

Then he took a bottle of sleeping pills there to escape into an eternal dream.

Then the current D took the body over.

D gave a moment of silence for Chu Feng.

Then he grinned even more when he realized where he was from the memories he saw.

He was in a Pokémon world.

It had been a long time since D enjoyed playing Pokémon but it was one of his favorite games.

He had played the games and watched the anime.

A ten-year-old could take a starter and travel this relaxed world like Ash.

Reality was bitter next to dreams but D was already grinning at the chance.

Those memories left D quiet.

He sighed.

His empty stomach answered with a loud gurgle.

He checked the time.

2:00 AM.

From the look of things, Chu Feng had skipped dinner.

D sighed and thanked him under his breath for the food, then got out of bed and opened the backpack from memory.

He found a pack of instant noodles, poured water into a pot, and set it on the coal stove.

While he waited for it to boil, he searched the cabin again.

Besides basic seasonings, there was nothing else to eat.

This was a ranch, but it had sat unused for years.

Weeds and loose stone owned the fields.

There were no vegetables, no fruit, and no eggs.

Older memories of the place still clung to him.

Once the ranch had been busy and full.

Neat rows of crops filled the fields.

Fruit hung heavy on the trees.

A Pokémon house held Miltank brought in from Johto and Wooloo from Galar.

Rows of hives stood on the grass outside.

Combee carried nectar home and turned it into clear golden honey every few days.

The water came to a boil.

D tore open the ramen pack and was about to drop the noodles in when new text cut across his thoughts.

[Instant Ramen: Low-quality basic food with little nutrition. Easy to cook, so outdoor workers and travelers like it. Effect unknown.]

He frowned.

That felt familiar.

The hard noodles softened in the boiling water.

He added seasoning.

He was not a great cook, only someone who had learned enough to feed himself alone and save money.

Soon the ramen was done.

He poured it into a bowl and sat at the desk to eat.

The familiar text returned.

[Oversalted Clear Soup Noodles (D-): Low-tier dish. Too much salt wiped out any bonus effect. It only fills the stomach, and too much of it makes you thirsty. Salt-loving Pokémon might like it. Rating: Poor, but not bad enough to make a Pokémon faint.]

D's brow twitched.

He knew that screen.

Not from his own life.

From Chu Feng’s.

It was the dish panel from Culinary Master, a cash-grab cooking game that Taiwanese developer had shipped.

Players cooked meals that gave characters stats, buffs, and strange side effects.

Grades ran from F- to SSS, set by ingredient rarity, quality, and skill.

D- was mediocre.

F- could slap on debuffs and get party members killed in a fight.

"What is this?" D muttered, thinking of the web novels he used to read. "Is this my cheat?"

He eyed the word Pokémon in the text, then lifted the bowl and ate.

Salt flooded his mouth.

D: "..."

Yes.

Too much salt.

It was still the only food left in the cabin, so he forced it down while he worked out what the ability meant.

If he cooked for Pokémon, the food could give them real bonuses.

Probably.

# 2: Chapter 2: Past Days at the Ranch

He could not test it yet.

He had no Pokémon, and the original had left him almost broke.

At three in the morning, with his stomach finally quiet, he faced his situation.

Crossing into this world was good news.

In his first life the sins of his ancestors left him with a debt that he could never repay on his own.

So he washed the dishes and sorted the backpack.

A wanderer’s kit was simple.

Spare clothes, three packs of dried noodles, and a scatter of coins and bills.

He counted about 2,300 League coins.

That would not fund a journey.

It might not even last a month.

So next to what he had carried before, it felt relaxed and comfortable.

Then he thought of the Pokémon from the anime, cute and fierce at once, and straightened up again.

“One step at a time.”

Maybe he had not slept before the crossing, or maybe this body was worn out.

After eating, sleep rolled over him.

He did not fight it.

No late shift tomorrow.

No rent to scrape for.

A knock woke him at dawn.

The knocking kept on.

Hair a mess, he opened the door.

Beside him stood a green lizard-like Pokémon on two legs.

Its hide shone in the morning light.

A tire-like ring sat on its neck.

A strong dark-green tail swayed behind it, tipped in clear white.

Big eyes studied D with open curiosity.

D knew it at once.

Cyclizar.

“You didn’t give me an answer about taking care of the ranch,” the mayor said. “I wanted to see if you had made up your mind.”

The mayor continued. “I figured the work would be new to you and cleaning alone is hard, so I brought this child along.”

Yesterday morning the original had run into Mayor Lewis on the way back.

Lewis had recognized him and said the ranch was the grandfather’s bequest.

He had cleaned it now and then over the years.

The house still worked.

Lewis and the grandfather had been friends.

He had even held D as a child.

Lewis had asked if he meant to stay and look after it.

The original had not given him a clear answer.

Kin gone.

Alone in the world.

So he had brought a young Cyclizar from his own home.

Company for the boy, and help for the land.

“Will you look after it for me?” Lewis asked.

D understood at once.

He looked at Cyclizar.

Cyclizar looked back, bright and curious.

In Paldea, Cyclizar had lived with people for ages.

They were mild, hated the cold, and liked a rider’s warmth on their backs.

They were transport as much as partners.

D had no reason to refuse.

He wanted a Pokémon of his own.

Getting a starter from a local professor like Ash was off the table.

He had no Poké Balls, and he was not going to punch wild Pokémon into friendship.

“Thank you. I’ll take good care of it.”

Lewis smiled. “Then he’s yours. First day on the ranch, you’re still new to the work, right?”

After a life of 9-to-9, all of this felt foreign.

“Yes.”

Roof, walls, and a place to start.

“Yes.”

“Ten years already. Time moves fast.”

He still saw the busy years in his mind.

Now the place was empty and broken.

Weeds and rock owned the fields.

The Pokémon house was a skeleton of its old shape.

They walked the grounds.

Cyclizar trailed behind and watched the young man in secret.

D listened hard.

He had decided to stay.

“That building is the house. The big box beside it is the shipping crate. Crops, milk, whatever you don’t need can go in there.”

“There’s a valley to the east. Gentle small Pokémon live there. You can forage berries and mountain goods, then put what you don’t keep in the crate.”

“I used to pick up items in the evening, but I don’t anymore. Now every day in the evening, someone from town picks up the crate and settles payment. The goods will go to Porto Marinada Market.”

“If you can scale the farm up you can even have your products shipped across Paldea.”

D blinked.

What was this, Harvest Moon?

He had played farming games, so he adapted fast.

He spotted a ruin ringed by trees and weeds.

“What’s that?”

“After he passed, your parents sold them off. The building went to ruin.”

“Don’t worry. Once you’re settled, the carpenter can expand the house and fix the Pokémon house.”

Lewis smiled. “Cyclizar’s strong. He’ll help.”

“Cy-cy~” Cyclizar chirped.

Lewis started to leave, then turned back.

“Keep the wood and stone you cut. When you have some money saved up, take them to the carpenter. You’ll save a lot on materials.”

D made sure to note that down.

From a short way off, Lewis tipped his hat. “I live in Porto Marinada. Come find me anytime. Cyclizar knows the way. Ride him there.”

“Alright.”

Lewis left.

League Year 298. Early spring in Paldea. The air was still cool.

D and Cyclizar watched him go, then D turned to the gleaming Cyclizar beside him.

Even as a beginner, he could tell the Pokémon was healthy and well raised.

“Cyclizar?”

“Cy?”

It knew its name.

“If you understand me, nod.”

“Cy-cy~”

Cyclizar nodded.

Same as the anime.

Pokémon could follow human speech.

Curiosity drove away other concerns.

Their minds and feelings were far deeper than ordinary animals.

Some matched and even exceeded that of people.

D touched the shiny hide.

It felt like leather, not scaly like a lizard.

More like a motorcycle.

Cyclizar leaned into the touch and squinted, pleased.

“Cyclizar, look after me from here on.”

“Cy-cy~”

D felt like he didn’t need to say anything else and leaned against Cyclizar.

His stomach growled.

Cyclizar looked at his middle.

“Have you eaten? I’m cooking. Want to join?”

“Cy-cy~”

Cyclizar had already had breakfast, but the invite still made it happy.

It followed him inside.

Looking at the cabin, D felt something shift.

This was home now.

Last life, 9-to-5.

This life, doing what he wanted would be fun.

Ranch work would be hard, but would be fun.

It was kind of like camping, just extended a lot.

New life starting.
