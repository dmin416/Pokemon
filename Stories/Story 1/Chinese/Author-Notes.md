# Author Notes / Non-Story Extras

English translation of useful author notes from `Pokemon Rancher Chinese.epub`. Volume 4 wrap-up and afterword are last. Official English Pokémon terms used where applicable. Protagonist kept as **D** (source name).

## 1. End of volume 1 + D’s Pokémon observation notes

Thank you to every reader who’s stuck with this.
Before I knew it, the book hit 320,000 words, and volume one is officially done.
I’ve been writing for years, but my craft still isn’t enough to handle a big cast well. A lot of the Pokémon end up vague, with no real memorable personality, and I’m not happy about that.
That’s a real regret. *self-reflection.jpg*
Across those 320,000 words the lead ran into a lot of fun scenes and a lot of different Pokémon. Sometimes while writing I’d smile at those images.
Sometimes I’d think the Pokémon world is so wonderful I’d love to get dropped into it myself.
Honestly, my desktop wallpaper is a landscape from the Isle of Armor. After a writing session I’ll stare at the Pokémon playing on it and daydream about what would happen if I crossed over.
So to feed that craving I wrote a lovable Dragonite, an adorable Pawmi, and I still want to write even more species.
I keep forcing myself to hold back: don’t add too many, you won’t be able to describe them all! Then readers will call you out… that “heart is willing, skill isn’t” feeling sucks.
I really envy writers who can make every side character distinct.
Next is volume two. The volume title is just **Summer**, covering the lead’s summer life.
There’s still a lot to do on the ranch, and Pokémon that haven’t evolved yet still need raising.
So besides more farming, volume two’s plots will grow out of those Pokémon.
Smoliv evolves into Arboliva, all the Nacli evolve into Garganacl, Pawmi evolves into Pawmot.
Haha, one thing I’m proud of: from launch until now I haven’t taken a single day off!
Maybe I can finally ditch the “king of leave requests” nickname.
That’s enough summary. What follows is the lead’s observation notes on the ranch Pokémon.

## 2. End of volume 8 (structure notes; leave note mixed in)

The weather’s been weird lately. I caught a chill, got dizzy and nauseous, threw up twice over the toilet. I didn’t want to take leave, but I couldn’t think straight.
Volume eight is done. Two volumes left: one “Moon,” then the final volume.
Eight volumes total: spring, summer, autumn, winter, then wind, flower, snow, moon.
“Wind-flower-snow-moon” usually means elegant beautiful scenery, but it isn’t only about beauty.
It also means seasons, time passing, how fragile life is. Personally I think **time** is a major element of this book.
From the lead’s crossing over and meeting Pokémon, to marrying and having kids, living through the seasons with Pokémon and family for a full life, the story cares a lot about time passing.
That approach is pretty niche for Pokémon fic. Almost nobody writes a lead’s whole life like this. Most Pokémon fics crown a Champion and end when the main plot wraps.
But the outline was set this way from the start. Without marriage and kids, later arcs can’t develop, and it even affects the ending.
As for romance: early on every day I’d see comments like “Pokémon fic only needs Pokémon, why a heroine,” “I drop it when I see a heroine,” “don’t write a heroine.”
So whenever I wrote the heroine I got timid, skimmed, avoided… and that made the romance feel bad now.
Some comments assume marriage and kids means the plot will only be about that. It won’t. There’s still a lot of mainline left.
Kalos Mega Evolution, Unbound Hoopa, the Galar Eternatus incident, and the time paradox running through the whole book.
Farming fics and Pokémon fics share a problem: after the early novelty fades and the word count piles up, everything feels ordinary. Late-game a lot of leads turn into “legendary guy” and lose the feel of living with ordinary Pokémon.
Compared to my last book I think I improved: that one got boring at 1.5 million words; this one only around 2 million.
Luckily we’re in the late game. Maybe 800,000 words left, maybe less.
Today is September 13, 2024. I’m aiming to finish around January 29, 2025, Chinese New Year’s Day.
Thanks for reading. No update today. One day off.

## 4. End of volume 4

Volume four is done. The book’s at about 900,000 words.
Before I started, I roughly split the story into three parts. Part one is complete.
D’s crossing, running the ranch, Koraidon arriving, building the ranch up with Pokémon… that’s all part one.
My one regret is how some Pokémon are characterized.
That’s just how Pokémon fic goes: catch too many and earlier ones become background. Regular readers know the feeling.
So I can only carefully write the main, important ones.
For example Koraidon, Cyclizar, Cubchoo, Dragonite, Pawmot, Gogoat, Calyrex…
Or maybe I just don’t write well enough (*sigh*)!
I envy authors who make every side character feel alive.
Back on topic: part one left a lot of open threads.
What the Great Adventurer team meets next on their travels;  
how Calyrex becomes king of Paldea;  
how Spectrier and Glastrier do in the racing festival;  
what the World Tree becomes as it grows, and which legendaries come to settle or train;  
Pawmot’s path to superhero fame;  
Dragonite Express dominating Paldea delivery;  
whether Shaymin gets drawn to Giratina’s remade Distortion World, and what happens between them;  
how Gogoat changes after the World Tree feeds power back;  
whether famous people from other regions (Champions, Elite Four) hear about the ranch and challenge the Grass Trial;  
Gimmighoul’s 999-coin road;  
the ghost of North Province;  
the classic “shock! my Champion ranch got exposed by Qishu” plot;  
I promise whatever I write stays centered on Pokémon.
And most important: the truth of Koraidon and Cyclizar’s time travel, what Cyclizar went through in the past, and how it returned to the present.
I was stuck on that until I finished *The Indigo Disk*. Then it clicked.
Oh, so that’s how it can work! Terapagos can play that kind of role!
That plot is core to the book. It probably only gets answered properly in part three.
At my update pace, around this time in 2024 you should be able to learn the truth.
From today, the book officially enters stage two.
I work a day job and write at night, so I don’t have much time to read other stuff, but I do see tips and comments.
Thank you so much for reading and supporting. Thanks for tips and monthly tickets!
This small flop author kneels in thanks orz

## 5. Afterword

It’s finished!
Started around June 2023, finished January 2025. A year and seven months. D’s story has reached its end.
Thank you for a year of support. Your comments and subscriptions carried this to the finish.
A lot of readers said this was the first book they ever fully subscribed to. This flop author is moved and a little panicked… thank you, truly.
I won’t claim it’s a perfect story, but I hope it moved you at least a little.
For a writer, that matters.
Especially seeing comments on the last two chapters: tears, sadness…
That made me happy, because it means the emotion landed.
Writing those two chapters yesterday, even I felt choked up. When the last word fell, I felt lost.
*Not Being a Trainer This Time* is done. D’s story is over. I can’t keep writing them anymore.
D, Koraidon, Pawmot, Dragonite, Calyrex… every Pokémon was shaped carefully. Saying goodbye hurts a bit.
I had the ending planned before the book launched, and I’d already written part of it.
I like locking the opening and ending first, then filling the middle.
That’s why I couldn’t end early: change the ending and the whole outline and logic collapse.
I also have to admit the book’s flaws.
Some scenes are overly sentimental and awkward; some stretch too long. It can feel strong at the start and weaker at the end.
That’s what I need to improve, including the leave-taking problem… next book I’ll try to fix it!
There’s still a sequel extra tomorrow: later developments for Tree’s House, some descendant coming to inherit great-great-grandpa’s ranch, that kind of thing.
D’s story is over, but a new one is starting.
Yes, the usual “new book” pitch!
Honestly I have two prepared.
I mentioned them in a leave note once; vote again if you want (leave a chapter comment on the one you’d read; I’ll check).

Don’t delete this book from your shelf yet; I’ll announce the new one here. Hope you’ll keep supporting this newbie.
That’s it for now. Ugh, D’s story is over and it really hurts. I won’t get to type that name the same way again.
